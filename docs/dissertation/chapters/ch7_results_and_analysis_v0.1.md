# Chapter 7 — Results and Analysis

## 7.1 Experiment E1: comparison of pre-trained models

Fifty-nine evaluation runs were recorded across three batches on 25 July 2026, and all results in this section were exported directly from the run store (Appendix B). No run failed outright: reliability was 1.0 for every completed model-task cell.

**[Table 7.1 — E1 model-level summary per batch: mean accuracy, mean latency, overall score, attempts and failures. Source: e1_export summary_by_model tables.]**

In the screening batch containing all three models, `qwen2.5:latest` and `qwen2.5-coder:latest` achieved mean accuracies of 0.86 (0.8627 and 0.8588 respectively) at mean latencies of 32.6 s and 40.9 s per task, while `deepseek-r1:8b` achieved 0.38 at 121.2 s on the only task it completed. The deepseek evaluation was curtailed after two test-plan runs because its reasoning traces made it approximately four times slower than the alternatives; its comparison is therefore confined to that task and no claim about its case-generation or coding ability is made. On this evidence the reasoning-oriented model was dominated on both axes for the platform's purposes, and the two Qwen variants were carried into the deep-metric batch.

The deep-metric batch exercised both Qwen models with four runs per task over two benchmark items. `qwen2.5:latest` obtained a higher overall score than the coder variant (0.783 against 0.733), a result that ran contrary to the initial expectation that a code-specialised model would dominate a testing workload. The per-task breakdown explains the reversal: on automation code both models achieved perfect deterministic accuracy (1.0) and identical hallucination cleanliness (1.0), but on test-case generation the general model was both more accurate (0.843 against 0.802) and substantially faster (57.7 s against 119.3 s), and on plan generation the two were close (0.673 against 0.643) with neither strong. Completeness and requirement coverage followed the same pattern: the general model reached 1.0 completeness on cases and automation against the coder's 0.967 and 0.60. Consistency across repetitions ranged from 74.7 to 99.7 (on a 0–100 scale) with no model uniformly more stable: the coder was near-deterministic on automation (99.7) but the general model was more consistent on cases (86.3 against 80.5).

**[Figure 7.1 — E1 research-score dimensions per model and task (deep-metric batch). Source: e1_export research_table.]**

Two properties of these results deserve emphasis. First, the deterministic hallucination metric recorded 1.0 (fully clean) for every cell in the deep-metric batch: at temperature 0.1, with grounded prompts, neither model emitted non-existent framework methods in this benchmark — which sharpens, rather than contradicts, the fine-tuning finding below. Second, response accuracy on test plans (0.64–0.67) was the weakest dimension for both models, indicating that the ten-section planning task, not code generation, was the hardest task for 7B-class models under the rubric.

The designed LLM-as-judge and satisfaction dimensions contain no data (zero rows in the store), and all judge-weighted rubric components were renormalised away; E1 is accordingly a deterministic-metric comparison, and qualitative dimensions such as readability were not measured.

## 7.2 Experiment E2: fine-tuning study

**Training behaviour.** Both adaptations trained 0.303 % of parameters (23.07 M of 7,615.6 M). The planner's validation loss fell monotonically apart from one transient rise — 0.470 at iteration 1 to 0.003 at the final iteration 270 — indicating a stable fit to the planning distribution. The coder behaved differently: validation loss reached its minimum of 0.026 after a single epoch (iteration 43) and then degraded — 0.032, 0.067, 0.043 and finally 0.054 at iteration 215 — a classic over-fitting trajectory on a 43-example corpus. The exported model was fused from the final checkpoint rather than the best one, so the deployed coder fine-tune embodied the over-fitted weights; earlier checkpoints were retained on disk but were not re-exported. Peak unified memory was 13.3 GB (planner) and 16.6 GB (coder); quantisation reduced each fused model from 14,526 MiB to 4,460 MiB.

**[Figure 7.2 — Training and validation loss per iteration for both adaptations; the coder's post-epoch-one rise is annotated. Source: data/f7_loss_curves.csv.]**

**Held-out evaluation.** On the unseen helpdesk application, evaluated end to end through the production agents (one run per cell), the planner fine-tune increased case production on two of three features (8→10 and 8→9 cases, one unchanged) and increased justified test-type selections in the plan from two to five, with both models completing all ten plan sections; its distinct-category count, however, moved in both directions (4→5, 4→5, but 7→3 on the search feature), and mean case-generation latency rose from 60.3 s to 66.6 s. The coder fine-tune improved every convention metric — files carrying pytest markers rose from 0/3 to 3/3, page-object emission from 0/3 to 3/3, and compilation from 1/3 to 2/3, with traceability comments, import resolution and forbidden-construct cleanliness already perfect in both — at the cost of tripled latency (15.0 s to 48.4 s per file).

**[Table 7.2 — E2 held-out before/after results per metric. Source: finetune/work/eval_report.json.]**

**Operational contradiction.** The held-out numbers, taken alone, suggest the coder fine-tune was an improvement. Operational evidence contradicted this: the project's own launch script was subsequently changed to prefer the *base* coder model by default, with an in-source justification that the fine-tune "hallucinates page-object methods and emits unparseable files often enough to exhaust the generation retry budget" in real platform use. This contradiction is treated as a first-class finding rather than an inconvenience. It is consistent with the training evidence — an over-fitted checkpoint may reproduce corpus conventions (markers, page-object structure) while degrading generalisation to unseen page-object APIs — and it demonstrates precisely the phenomenon RQ2 asks about: a model that *looks* better on a narrow measured slice, and even in casual use, behaved worse under the deployed pipeline's deterministic gates, which is only visible because those gates measure rejection. Platform-level per-model runs (login, administrator and product features) were performed through the deployed system and would triangulate this finding; their database export is pending [EVIDENCE REQUIRED: PostgreSQL export of per-model projects].

A planned comparison of the fine-tuned models under the full E1 metric framework was not performed — the two evaluation strands were never joined — so no rubric-comparable before/after scores exist, and none are reported.

## 7.3 Experiment E3: system-level validation

**Generated suites.** Twenty-one automation modules were generated, approved and retained: eight user-interface, five API, three regression-API and two regression-UI files, plus three early files predating the marker scheme, with generation provenance recorded by content hash and timestamp. All twenty-one carried machine traceability headers linking tests to case and requirement identifiers.

**Execution evidence.** One hundred and seven execution-run directories were retained from local runs between 1 and 15 August 2026 (105 with JUnit reports, alongside pytest logs, screenshots and traces). The final preflight verification (15 August) confirmed all four services healthy, workspace integrity intact and both grounding sources live (page distillation and a 32-endpoint OpenAPI parse), and executed the approved suites of six platform projects: the principal sample-application suite ran green (7 passed, 0 failed across four files), while two other projects were partially passing (8 passed/2 failed; 3 passed/1 failed), and one cloud-configured project failed on an unreachable service. End-to-end operation was thus demonstrated, but not uniformly green across all configured projects, and this is reported as observed.

**Failure realism.** The retained failures were substantively informative rather than infrastructural. One representative JUnit report records a Playwright strict-mode violation — a generated locator matching three product rows — which is exactly the ambiguity class the unmerged locator-scanner branch was designed to eliminate. Conversely, the retained suites include generated defects that passed the gates: a file with two sixty-second fixed waits and hard-coded credentials (evading the sleep and secret checks, which matched different patterns), and a file whose tests always skipped due to a truthiness error, masking a missing import and a malformed selector. Gate efficacy is therefore demonstrable in both directions, and these artefacts are reproduced in Appendix F.

**Continuous integration.** The five-suite, marker-selected pipeline with a regression gate against the last successful main-branch baseline was fully specified, and its gate logic was unit-tested; however, the workflow file was deleted from the main branch by the project's final commit, and no run artefact from the hosted pipeline was preserved. CI execution therefore remains unevidenced [EVIDENCE REQUIRED: GitHub Actions run listing or screenshots], and the pipeline is claimed as designed and locally validated only. The seeded-defect detection experiment likewise remained unexecuted at the time of writing; the three defect toggles are in place and the procedure is defined in Section 6.4.

## 7.4 Summary of findings

Across the three experiments the verified evidence supports four findings: (i) among locally hosted candidates, the general 7B instruct model was the strongest overall platform default, and the reasoning model was unsuitable on latency alone; (ii) fine-tuning reliably taught convention adherence but, on the coder task, over-fitted a small corpus and degraded real-world generalisation — a divergence detectable only through deployed, deterministic measurement; (iii) the governed pipeline demonstrably produced, gated and executed real suites against a live application, with honest partial failures; and (iv) the deterministic gates caught the hallucination classes they encoded while missing pattern variants they did not, motivating the defence-in-depth design rather than any single gate.
